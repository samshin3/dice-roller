### Graph Problem (STC 0040)

Six towns are joined by two-way roads (distances in km):

| Road | Distance (km) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1–2 | 3 |
| 2–3 | 3 |
| 1–3 | 4 |
| 4–5 | 3 |
| 5–6 | 3 |
| 4–6 | 4 |
| 3–6 | 14 |
| 1–4 | 14 |
| 3–4 | 18 |
| 2–5 | 20 |
| 1–5 | 25 |

A salesman starts at town 1, visits every town exactly once, and returns to town 1. What is the shortest total distance? Write the answer as a four-digit number.

### Logic Riddle

Six islanders, A through F, are each either a Knight (always tells the truth) or a Knave (always lies). They say:

* **A:** "B and E are the same type."
* **B:** "C is a knave, or D is a knave (or both)."
* **C:** "If B is a knight, then E is a knight."
* **D:** "The number of knaves among the six of us is even."
* **E:** "A and F are both knaves."
* **F:** "A is a knight and E is a knave."

**Question:** Who is a knight, and who is a knave? (There is exactly one consistent answer.)

> **Hint:** Start with A and split into cases. One case collapses quickly, and D's statement about parity is what resolves the other.

**Answer:** Knights: A, C, D, F. Knaves: B, E.

### Text Riddle

I learn best when someone hands me the answers.  
Every example I study comes with a label, a "correct" response sitting right beside it.  
I guess, I get graded, and I adjust until my guesses match the labels.  
Ask me to sort emails into spam or not spam, or to predict a house price from its features, and I'm in my element.  
Take the labels away, and I have nothing to learn from.
